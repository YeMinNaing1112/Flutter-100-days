# In The Circle — Discovery Session Notes

> **Status:** Early discovery. These are working hypotheses and founder reflections, not validated findings about couples in general. Keep exploring before deciding on features.

## Original idea

The original note describes the app broadly as being for long-distance relationships. The idea may also apply to couples who live in the same city or together.

## Initial conversation with partner

### What we discussed

- When both partners are busy, it can feel understandable because they know they are both working hard for a better future.
- When one partner is busy and the other is free, the free partner may feel they are not getting enough attention.

### Early insight

The difficulty may not simply be busyness. A mismatch in availability may leave one partner wanting connection while the other cannot give it at that moment.

This comes from one conversation and is not evidence that all couples experience the same problem.

### Still to explore

- What does “enough attention” mean to the partner who feels they are missing it?
- Is the unmet need communication, quality time, feeling remembered, emotional connection, reassurance, or something else?
- How often does this happen, how much does it affect the relationship, and how do couples currently handle it?
- Do other couples describe the same difficulty?

**Question to return to later:** When one partner is busy and the other is free, what does the free partner feel is missing when they say they are not getting enough attention?

## Emerging product direction — founder reflection

### Intended outcome

The founder wants In The Circle to help couples maintain commitment and feel more involved in each other's everyday lives—not only couples in long-distance relationships.

### Outcomes the founder cares about

- Understand each other's daily lives better and have fewer misunderstandings.
- Feel emotionally closer even when they cannot talk.
- Feel more involved in each other's lives by sharing everyday moments.
- Create small moments of connection throughout the day without requiring long conversations.
- Help partners feel that both people are making an effort to maintain the relationship.

**Most important intended outcome so far:** Help partners feel and demonstrate commitment through mutual effort and involvement.

### Target couple hypothesis

Couples who genuinely want their relationship to work but struggle because of busy lives or different schedules.

The main target is not a relationship where one person simply does not care; the working hypothesis is that both partners care but have difficulty turning that care into everyday involvement.

### Possible contributing problems identified by the founder

The founder suspects that couples may struggle because:

1. They have different expectations about how much attention or effort the relationship needs.
2. They struggle to share their daily lives and gradually feel disconnected.
3. They intend to spend time together but do not turn those intentions into actual plans.

These are hypotheses to investigate, not confirmed root causes.

### What “involvement” means so far

The founder wants partners to both:

- Know what the other person is doing in daily life.
- Share the experiences and stories behind those activities.

Of the following, **mutual participation feels slightly more important** to the founder:

- **Mutual sharing:** Both share daily plans, activities, and experiences.
- **Mutual understanding:** Both understand each other's responsibilities, feelings, and expectations.
- **Mutual participation:** Both take an active interest in each other's lives and make an effort to stay involved.

This distinction is useful for discovery, but it does not yet establish what the product should build.

## Next question for the next session

Imagine two partners share their daily schedules and know what each other is doing. What behaviors would make that sharing feel like real commitment rather than just exchanging information?

- **A. Taking initiative:** Both independently initiate sharing, ask about each other's day, and make plans.
- **B. Responding with interest:** When one shares something, the other makes an effort to respond and show genuine interest.
- **C. Following up:** Each remembers important things their partner mentioned and asks about them later.
- **D. Making time together:** Both notice opportunities in their schedules and actively arrange time to connect.

Choose as many as matter, then identify which matters most.

## Research reminder

Do not jump from the desired outcome (commitment) straight to features (such as schedule sharing). Investigate what couples actually experience, what they currently do, and what behaviors make them feel cared for. A product may support willing partners in demonstrating commitment; it cannot create genuine commitment by itself.
